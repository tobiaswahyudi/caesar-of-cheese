# Naming Rules
- Terms
	- tuple names
		- 2 to 4: "Double", "Triple", Quadruple".
		- Tuples 5 and above will be called "n-tuple".
        - 1-tuples do not receive a prefix.
    - Frequency
		- 1 or <33%: "a bit of"
		- 5+ or >50%: "a lot of"
		- in between: "some"
        - Quantities that satisfy multiple frequency names may be resolved freely as the server's judgment.
- Lettuce
    - A stack with lettuce will be names with the following procedure:
        - Record the number of lettuce -> V
        - Remove the lettuce from the stack.
        - Record the number of ingredients in the remaining stack -> R
        - Determine the name of the remaining stack -> S
		- The name of the original stack will be "S with n Lettuce"
            - where n is the frequency term resolved with the quantity V or the ratio V/R.
- Base cases: length 1 and 0
	- An empty stack will be called "Nothing".
    - For naming purposes, TopBun is considered equivalent to Bun.
    - All single ingredient stacks will be named its single ingredient.
	- Ingredients are: "Bun", "Patty", "Lettuce", "Cheese".
- Base cases: Ubiquitous
	- Stacks of the shape B P*n B will be called an "n-tuple Hamburger".
	- Stacks of the shape B (PC)*n B or B (CP)*n B will be called an "n-tuple Cheeseburger".
		- "n-tuple Cheeseburger"s do not allow mixed orders of Patty and Cheese. BPCCPB is not considered a "Double Cheeseburger".
	- Stacks of the shape B C*n B will be called an "n-tuple Cheese Sandwich".
		- It's tempting to have this supersede the "Cheese Burger" title. But it's not what customers expect a Cheeseburger to be.
- Structural rules
    - If a stack does not resolve into base cases, we apply structural rules. These may be applied in any order.
        - Burger Mathematicians have proven that we can name any burger with these.
        - However, 'bad names' may result. For more details on 'bad naming', consult the Extended Employee's Guide section 36.
        - Remember that naming quality will reflect on employee performance reviews.
	- we denote by "name(S)" the name of a stack S.
	- A stack with the shape B S B is named a "name(S) Burger".
	- A stack with the shape S*n is named an "n-tuple name(S)".
	- A stack with the shape XY is named a "name(X) and name(Y)".

# Serving Rules
- the thing you serve must always be a burger or a sandwich.
- If a frequency is not specified by the customer, quantity may be resolved freely as the server's judgment.
- Customers must be satisfied!
    - You may have to explain why a burger is named as such.
